## 1. Messaging Pillars (use consistently across all copy)

1. **"The next horizon."** This is a graduation, not a pitch. Everyone in the warmest audience already completed find (webinar) and is mid-focus (workshop). Certification is form — the next stage in a journey they're already on, not a new decision imposed on them. Every waitlist-facing hook should read as continuation, never as a cold ask.
2. **The five-stage journey — find, focus, form, follow, fruition.** The certification's job is explicit: teach people to *guide others* through this same arc. Frame the credential as "you found your own direction — now you're trained to help someone else find theirs." Never expose the 26-concept framework or Concept Dictionary — five stages only.
3. **Chronos vs. Kairos, applied to *this* decision.** Use sparingly and only where it adds real meaning — e.g., "A credential is a Chronos thing — a date, a certificate. What you do with it is Kairos." Do not force this pillar into every hook; it works best in waitlist/nurture copy where the audience already knows the term from the webinar.
4. **Purpose Fingerprint™, at one remove.** Individual-credential copy sells *your own* Purpose Fingerprint deepening into a guiding practice. Corporate-tier copy does not use this term the same way — it should never be used to suggest the license conveys DrG's own Purpose Fingerprint or founder-level authority (see brand-boundary note in Section 4).
5. **Teach what changed you.** The through-line connecting individual and small-scale angles: the strongest converts are people who were moved by their own find/focus experience and want a structured, credentialed way to do that for others — not people shopping for a generic coaching certification. Angles should sell the *facilitation practice*, not a credential-as-credential.
6. **Differentiation without dismissal (new).** Purposeology is entering a real, populated category, not an empty one — named competitors include True Purpose® Institute (secular, proprietary-method), Called Coach Institute (faith-based), and Calling Coach Academy (secular, "translate calling into career/life reinvention"). No angle should imply "nothing else like this exists." Differentiate on three real, defensible points instead: (a) faith-adjacent-without-denominational framing — a lane distinct from both the fully secular competitors and Called Coach Institute's explicitly faith-based positioning; (b) dual-audience reach into veteran and corporate/L&D populations, which none of the named competitors appear built for; (c) the Corporate Delivery License tier, a structural offering none of the named competitors appear to have. Comparisons should read as "here's what this specifically gives you" rather than naming competitors in consumer-facing copy.
7. **Legitimacy is earned, shown, not asserted (new).** Accreditation/legitimacy skepticism is the single most consistently cited objection in the research — ahead of price, time investment, or market-saturation concerns, and rooted in a real fact: coaching is an unregulated industry, and no accreditor certifies a "purpose coaching" sub-specialty as its own credential (competitors' "ICF-accredited" claims refer to general coach-training hours, not a purpose-specific credential). Do not claim or imply a third-party accreditation Purposeology doesn't have. Instead, every full-launch angle set should include copy that answers "is this real?" with specifics — what the assessment is, who reviews it, that DrG is directly involved — not a vague reassurance.

## 2. Pilot-Cohort Angles (small, founding-cohort framing — use only if the user selects the pilot path)

Distinct tone from full-launch: intimate, limited, hand-selected, "help us build this" rather than "enroll in our program." No LMS/licensing language, no polished-program promises the spec says don't exist yet (no formal assessment rubric, no directory infrastructure).

- **Waitlist email subject:** "You're One of the First People I'm Asking About This"
  - Hook: positions the recipient as hand-picked, not mass-marketed to; honest about it being new ("we're building this together, not handing you a finished program").
- **Waitlist email subject:** "Before This Becomes a School, It's Going to Be a Small Room"
  - Hook: names the pilot explicitly as small and founding-cohort — sets honest expectations rather than overselling infrastructure that doesn't exist yet.
- **Landing/waitlist-page headline (pilot version):** "The First Certified Purposeologists — Founding Cohort"
  - Hook: frames scarcity as real (genuinely 10–20 seats) rather than manufactured.
- **Social post (waitlist-only, not public):** "You went through find. You're mid-focus. I'm opening a small door into form — for the first group, before this is a full program. Reply if you want in."
- **Application-style CTA (not "enroll," since assessment isn't designed yet):** "Apply for the Founding Cohort" rather than "Register" — accurate to the offer spec's "informal assessment by DrG personally" stage, and sets a selective, credible tone for what is currently an unproven credential.
- **Accreditation/legitimacy pre-empt, pilot-specific (new):** Waitlist email subject: "This Isn't an Accredited Badge — It's Direct Training, Direct Assessment, From Me"
  - Hook: turns the pilot's actual shape (small, personally assessed by DrG, no third-party accreditation body) into the trust signal itself, rather than hoping the "is this legit" question doesn't come up. Honest framing here doubles as differentiation, since it's a credible claim a mass-market, LMS-delivered competitor program can't make as convincingly.

**Channel notes:** email to the existing waitlist only; no paid social, no landing page indexed for cold traffic, no corporate one-pager. This set should not be published anywhere a stranger could stumble into a certification promise the program can't yet back up (no LMS, no formal assessment, no directory).

**Audience fit:** individuals in transition who completed the workshop/webinar funnel — the explicit "natural first cohort" the offer spec names. Secondary: faith-adjacent leaders and veterans already warm from workshop attendance, if any are in the founding-cohort-sized pool. Do not extend to cold psychology-adjacent institutional outreach or corporate/L&D at pilot scale — those relationships need a credential with real assessment behind it first.

## 4. Corporate Delivery License — Separate One-Pager (Fortune 500 L&D pitch)

Per the role brief and offer spec, this is a **distinct pitch, not a blend with individual-credential marketing.** Do not use "become certified" consumer language here; this is a B2B licensing/staffing pitch to L&D buyers, tied to priority #4 (Fortune 500 corporate training) rather than to individual transformation.

- **One-pager headline:** "A Licensed Delivery Bench for Purpose-Driven Training at Scale"
  - Hook: speaks to the buyer's real constraint — DrG cannot personally staff enterprise-scale engagements; a licensed practitioner bench can.
- **One-pager headline (meaning-gap angle, per the research report's finding that L&D buyers are sold skills/productivity almost exclusively — a meaning/retention angle is open territory):** "Your People Have the AI Skills. Do They Have a Reason to Use Them?"
  - Use only in the corporate one-pager, not in consumer-facing copy — this line targets a buyer's org-wide retention/engagement concern, not an individual's personal-purpose search.
- **Subhead / positioning line:** "Licensed Purposeologists, Trained and Quality-Controlled, Delivering a Defined Purpose-and-Leadership Format Inside Your Organization."
- **Differentiation line (new):** "No comparable purpose/calling-certification program in the market currently offers an organization-wide delivery license — this is a defined structural gap, and copy can lean on that white space without naming competitors directly."
- **CTA framing:** "Schedule a Conversation About Licensed Delivery" — not "enroll," not "certify your team" (the license is for delivery rights, not a bulk individual-certification sale; conflating the two risks selling something not yet defined).
- **Pricing framing (now safe to use, updated 2026-07-14):** The offer spec sets the Corporate Delivery License at **$12,000/year, scaling to $25,000+/year for mid-market accounts (50–200 seats)**, billed as a recurring annual license. This range may now be referenced in one-pager copy and direct outreach (e.g., "Licensing starts at $12,000/year and scales with account size") — the pricing decision itself is no longer open. Do **not**, however, state or imply royalty percentages, revenue-share terms, per-seat mechanics, or contract length beyond "annual license" — the offer spec is explicit that the underlying licensing/legal framework (what exactly the license grants, quality-control enforcement mechanics) is still undesigned. Treat the dollar figures as usable; treat the legal/economic structure behind them as still undecided.

**Brand-boundary note for this tier specifically:** copy for this one-pager must not imply the license conveys DrG's personal authority, credentials, or Purpose Fingerprint — it conveys rights to deliver a bounded, defined training format under quality control. Pricing may now be referenced (see above); royalty terms, revenue-share structure, and exact licensing scope have not been decided and should not be invented or implied in outreach copy.

**Audience fit:** corporate/L&D buyers exclusively (Fortune 500 L&D per priority #4; internal trainers/HR-L&D staff as a secondary "seed the org" angle per the offer spec). Not for individuals-in-transition, faith-adjacent, veteran, or psychology-adjacent segments — those are the core-tier audiences in Sections 2–3.

**Channel notes:** standalone PDF/one-pager and direct outreach only (sales conversations, warm intros via DrG's existing corporate contacts) — not email/social to the certification waitlist, and not the public landing page. Keep entirely separate from consumer-facing certification marketing per the role brief's explicit instruction.

Recommend using Section 2 (pilot) angles first regardless, since the offer spec's own recommendation is a proof point before full build-out, and Section 2 works whether or not the Council later approves the full school.
- **Pricing is now set — this item is resolved and no longer blocks price-bearing copy.** The offer spec (updated 2026-07-14) sets the core credential at **$3,495 (or 3 × $1,225 payment plan)** and the Corporate Delivery License at **$12,000/year scaling to $25,000+/year** for mid-market accounts. Note the offer spec's own caveat: these are data-informed prices set inside a validated competitive range, not lab-tested — real conversion confirmation still depends on the recommended pilot cohort. Practically: price-bearing CTAs, payment-plan mentions, and the corporate one-pager's dollar figures can now be drafted once the pilot-vs-full-launch decision (above) clears them for use — pricing readiness is no longer the blocker, launch-path readiness is.
- **Assessment/credentialing rigor is undesigned.** Full-launch angles (Section 3) that use the word "certified" or "credential" assume a real assessment bar exists by the time that copy runs — flag to confirm the assessment design is in place (or credibly in progress) before publishing Section 3 publicly, so the credential claim isn't made before it's earned. This is now also the substance behind the accreditation/legitimacy pre-empt angles (Sections 2 and 3.B) — those angles only work if there's a real, specific assessment process to point to; don't publish them ahead of that being true.
- **Corporate Delivery License legal/economic structure (royalty vs. flat fee, quality-control enforcement mechanics) is still undecided**, even though the headline dollar figures are now set. The one-pager in Section 4 can now state the $12,000–$25,000+/year range but should continue to avoid stating or implying royalty percentages, revenue-share terms, or per-seat mechanics until that structure is decided.
- **Competitive positioning is now grounded in confirmed named competitors** (True Purpose® Institute, Called Coach Institute, Calling Coach Academy) rather than an assumed-empty field — this is reflected in the updated pillars (Section 1) and angles (Sections 3.B and 4) above. No further user decision needed here unless the user wants competitors named explicitly in outward copy, which this brief does not recommend (differentiation is written implicitly, per Section 1, pillar 6).
