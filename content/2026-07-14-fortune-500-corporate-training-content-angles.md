## Scope Note and Governing Constraint

**There is no standalone cold enterprise campaign to write angles for, and this brief does not produce one.** Per the offer spec, this priority has no pilot account, no case study, no ROI/retention data, and no MSA/procurement infrastructure yet. Every angle below is written for one of two real, sequenced moments:

1. **The conversation after a keynote booking** (or an existing warm relationship) — an L&D/HR contact who has already seen DrG deliver, now considering a Discovery/Executive Session. This is the primary, actionable angle set given no cold campaign is planned.
2. **A one-pager for use once that conversation is genuinely underway** — i.e., a prospect has expressed real interest, not a leave-behind for cold prospecting.

Nothing here is written for paid ads, cold LinkedIn outreach, a public landing page, or mass email — those would misrepresent the offer's actual stage and are explicitly out of scope per the offer spec's own pilot-first recommendation.

## Messaging Pillars

1. **The unclaimed layer.** 2026 corporate AI-training spend is real and enormous ($400B+, per Josh Bersin) but almost entirely skills/productivity framed (72% cite efficiency, 70% cite future-skills prep). Virtually no one is training leaders on *why* their organization is adopting AI, only *how*. This is the one sourced, defensible claim this offer has — it should anchor every piece of content, not a manufactured proof point.
2. **Chronos vs. Kairos, applied to adoption.** Every AI rollout runs on a Chronos timeline — a go-live date, a training schedule, a completion metric. Almost none of them answer the Kairos question: what is this rollout *for*, right now, for this team. This is the organizational register version of the brand's signature reframe.
3. **The five-stage journey as an organizational lens.** Find, focus, form, follow, fruition — reused exactly as the offer spec frames it: find (what is this AI rollout actually for), focus/form (a team-specific articulation of purposeful adoption, delivered as a concrete artifact), follow (a sustained cadence, multi-session tier only), fruition (a stronger connection between AI-adoption effort and personal/team meaning — a design intent, not a measured outcome, and content should never imply otherwise).
4. **Leaders bring a Purpose Fingerprint™ into the room.** At the Leadership Training Day tier specifically, where the audience is a defined cohort of individual director-level-and-above leaders (not an anonymous mass rollout), the Purpose Fingerprint™ concept applies naturally at the individual-leader level: each leader's unrepeatable convergence of timing, context, and identity shapes how *they* lead adoption for their team. Use sparingly and only where the audience is a small, named leadership cohort — not for org-wide or mass-training framing, where it would overreach.
5. **Honest about the stage.** This offer has no case studies, no retention metrics, no client roster yet — and the content should not pretend otherwise. The honest framing ("you'd be shaping this, not buying a finished product") is itself a legitimate, differentiated pitch for the right kind of early buyer, not a weakness to paper over. See Flag 1 below on how far to take this in outward language.

## A. Post-Keynote Conversation Angle Set (Primary)

This is what actually gets used, per the offer spec's inbound-feed logic: a corporate/organizational booker or attendee at a DrG keynote (see the keynote content brief's "From Information to Formation: Leading Purpose in the Age of AI" topic, already built as the natural bridge) reaches out, or DrG's team follows up, to continue the conversation toward a Discovery/Executive Session. Tone: conversational, low-commitment, peer-to-peer — not a sales pitch and not a training catalog.

### Follow-up note / email hooks (from DrG or team, to the L&D/HR contact)

1. **"You just heard the 60-minute version. Here's the two-question version for your calendar."**
   Segues directly from a delivered keynote (especially the "From Information to Formation" topic) into an invitation, without restating the keynote or pitching a product. Best opener for a same-week follow-up.
2. **"Your AI training budget answers 'how.' Who's answering 'why'?"**
   Direct, conversational restatement of the meaning-gap thesis — works as a subject line or opening line, not a headline claim.
3. **"$400 billion is going into AI training this year. Almost none of it is about why your people should care."**
   Leads with the sourced Bersin figure as a shared observation, not a sales claim — invites a conversation rather than asserting a solution.
4. **"What happens after the rollout is done?"**
   Names the gap the skills-training wave leaves behind — training ends at go-live; the meaning question starts there. Works as a talking point for an in-person follow-up conversation at the event itself, not just email.
5. **"A working conversation, not a proposal."**
   Sets accurate expectations for what a Discovery/Executive Session actually is — a diagnostic working session, explicitly not a sales deck or training catalog. Useful as the literal framing line when DrG or a rep describes the next step out loud.

### Channel notes
- Email/LinkedIn follow-up sent within days of a keynote, addressed personally by DrG or a named team member — not an automated sequence.
- In-person talking points for DrG or staff at the event itself (registration desk, post-keynote mingling) — angles 1 and 4 work best spoken, not written.
- None of these are written for cold outreach to organizations that have not seen DrG speak or do not have an existing relationship — that would contradict the offer spec's explicit sequencing.

## B. Executive One-Pager (For Use Once a Real Conversation Is Underway)

For use after a prospect has expressed genuine interest — e.g., they've agreed to or are seriously weighing a Discovery/Executive Session. Register: CHRO/VP-L&D-facing, organizational, sober. No pricing stated (see Flag 3). No case studies, testimonials, or retention/ROI figures anywhere on this page — none exist.

### Headline options

1. **"The Meaning Gap in Your AI Rollout"**
   Direct, names the finding as the organization's problem, not DrG's pitch. Strongest as the page's primary headline.
2. **"Skills Get People Ready. Meaning Keeps Them."**
   Plain-language restatement of the retention-adjacent thesis — careful not to promise a retention outcome, only to frame the distinction (see Flag 4 on this exact line).
3. **"Your Rollout Runs on a Timeline. Does It Run on a Reason?"**
   Chronos/Kairos applied directly to the one-pager's opening hook.
4. **"A Working Session, Not a Training Catalog"**
   Reinforces the register constraint as a page section header — sets expectations for what the Discovery/Executive Session actually is before describing it.

### Body section angles (titles/hooks only, not full copy)

- **"The Finding"** — states the Bersin $400B figure and the 72%/70% skills/productivity framing plainly, sourced, with no embellishment. This section does the credibility work the offer currently has to do without a case study.
- **"What We're Proposing"** — describes the three tiers at the level of what each *is* (a diagnostic working session; a full-day leadership cohort engagement; a sustained multi-session program), explicitly not as a menu with prices attached.
- **"Why DrG, Personally"** — an honest credibility section: at this stage, DrG delivers every engagement personally — no delivery bench, no licensed-practitioner network yet. Framed as a founder-led-delivery advantage rather than a scale limitation (a legitimate, defensible framing per the offer spec's own Delivery Model rationale) — see Flag 1 for how directly to say this.
- **"What This Builds Toward"** — a forward-looking close naming the *follow*-stage cadence the multi-session tier is designed to sustain, worded as design intent ("built to sustain," "designed to produce") rather than a result claim.

### Channel notes
- Leave-behind PDF or slide-deck-style one-pager for an in-progress Discovery/Executive Session conversation, delivered directly by DrG or a named team member, never distributed as a downloadable asset on a public page.
- Royal Purple #4B0082 / Gold #FFD700 only; "DrG Alfred Palmer," no credentials, no "Dr." forms; DMV referenced only if genuinely relevant to on-site delivery logistics for the specific prospect.

## Audience Fit Notes

This offer has a single buyer register — organizational, not individual-calling — distinct from every other offer in this pipeline:

- **Primary buyer: CHRO, VP/Head of Talent Development, VP/Head of L&D.** All angles above are written to this reader — someone accountable for a leadership/culture budget line, not a technical AI-skills buyer. No "find your purpose" or individual-calling language appears anywhere in Section A or B; the five-stage journey and Chronos/Kairos pillars are both reframed at the team/organizational level throughout.
- **Champion/entry point: an internal L&D program manager or CHRO-adjacent exec** who has already seen the AI-skills-training wave inside their org and is looking for the retention/engagement angle it doesn't cover. The post-keynote follow-up set (Section A) is written to land with this person specifically — someone who just watched a keynote and is now the one who has to decide whether to keep the conversation going internally.
- **End participants: director-level-and-above leadership cohorts**, not front-line or mass-rollout populations — this is why the Purpose Fingerprint™ pillar (Section, Messaging Pillars #4) is usable at the Leadership Training Day tier specifically, where the audience is small and named, and should not be extended to org-wide messaging.
- **Not this offer's audience:** individuals in transition, faith-adjacent/spiritual-but-not-religious, veterans, and psychology-adjacent institutions are all named brief segments but do not apply to this content set — this offer speaks to one segment only ("corporate/organizational buyers — leadership training, Fortune 500 L&D"), consistent with the offer spec's own scoping. If a pilot account is eventually sourced through the veteran-services or psychology-adjacent relationship channels the offer spec names as candidate pilot paths, the *lead source* may differ, but the buyer register in the actual sales conversation stays CHRO/L&D-facing, not individual-framed.

## Flags for User Decision

1. **How directly to state the pre-pilot stage to a real prospect.** The "Why DrG, Personally" section (B) and Messaging Pillar #5 frame the lack of a case study/delivery bench as an honest, even favorable, founder-led-delivery story rather than hiding it. This is consistent with the offer spec's own Delivery Model rationale, but the exact language ("you'd be shaping this, not buying a finished product," "no case study yet, and here's why that's actually the offer") needs the user's read before it goes in front of a real CHRO — some buyers will read this as refreshingly honest, others may read it as underselling. Recommend testing the framing verbally in the first live conversation before it's fixed in a written one-pager.
2. **Whether "Fortune 500" belongs in any prospect-facing title.** No pilot account exists yet, and the first real engagement is more likely mid-size or large-but-not-F500 (per the offer spec's own "Recommended Minimal Proof Point"). Recommend keeping "Fortune 500" as an internal/planning label only and using generic "enterprise leadership" or "your leadership team" language in anything a real prospect sees, so the content never implies a scale or client tier that doesn't exist yet.
3. **No pricing language appears anywhere above, by design** — the offer spec's tiered pricing table is explicitly a placeholder, not user-confirmed. If the user wants a soft placeholder line on the one-pager (e.g., "investment scoped following the Discovery Session," a standard practice for undefined-scope engagements), that's a small, low-risk addition to make once approved — but it should not default to appearing without a decision, since even a vague pricing gesture is a public-facing claim about the offer's maturity.
4. **"Skills get people ready. Meaning keeps them."** — this headline option (B.2) reads close to a retention-outcome claim even though it's phrased as a distinction, not a result. Recommend the user confirm this specific line is safe, or drop it in favor of headline options 1, 3, or 4, which don't carry the same risk of being read as an implied retention promise.
5. **Purpose Fingerprint™ at the leadership-cohort level is a new application** of a term used elsewhere only at the individual-calling level. The framing above restricts it to small, named leadership cohorts (Leadership Training Day tier) specifically to avoid diluting the term — worth a quick confirmation that this extension reads as consistent brand usage rather than overreach.
