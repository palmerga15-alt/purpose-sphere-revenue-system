## 0. Framing note read from the offer spec before writing anything

This is the single most sensitive piece of copy in the pipeline. The product being marketed is explicitly **not** a generative "AI finds your purpose" tool — it is a guide that walks users through DrG Alfred Palmer's existing five-stage framework (find, focus, form, follow, fruition), grounded only in his published/delivered content, with fruition always handed off to a human. Every angle below is written to be true to that constraint, not just compliant with it on a technicality. Where an angle could be read as implying the AI originates or completes someone's purpose, it has been rejected rather than softened.

---

## 1. Messaging Pillars

These five should recur, in some form, across every piece of purposeology.ai content — landing page, widget copy, email, social, and any future deck reference.

1. **Conduit, Not Source.** The Guide is a doorway to DrG's framework and teaching — never the origin, and never the final word, on anyone's purpose. This is the load-bearing pillar; every other pillar and every angle below should be checked against it before it ships.
2. **Five Stages, One Process.** Find, focus, form, follow, fruition — the shared spine of every offer in the pipeline, now available as an always-on conversational companion between live events, not a replacement for them.
3. **Information vs. the Reason to Use It.** Direct extension of the active webinar thesis ("AI gives you information. Purposeology™ gives you the reason to use it.") — the Guide organizes and surfaces DrG's existing teaching; it does not replace the human meaning-making work the framework exists to do.
4. **Fruition Is a Conversation, Not a Click.** The culmination of the journey is, by design, a human handoff — to DrG/team now, to certified Purposeologists later. No angle should ever imply the Guide "finishes" someone's purpose-work.
5. **Built From What DrG Has Already Taught.** The Guide's knowledge base is DrG's own books, webinar/workshop content, and the public five-stage framework — not generic AI knowledge. This is a trust/differentiation pillar as much as a technical fact: it's a curated body of teaching, not an open-ended chatbot.

## 3. Titles / Hooks — Widget Intro / First Message (in-product, user-facing inside the tool)

These are **not** finished system-prompt copy (that's a dev/prompt-engineering deliverable per the offer spec's Dependencies section) but illustrative language showing the register the in-widget voice should hold — measured, self-identifying, boundary-stating from the first message. See Section 6 for why this differs from landing-page language.

- *"I'm a guide trained on DrG Alfred Palmer's Purposeology framework — the five-stage process of find, focus, form, follow, and fruition. I can help you work through where you are in that process. I won't tell you what your purpose is; that's not something I'm built to hand you."*
- *"Where are you starting from tonight — find, focus, form, or follow? Tell me what's on your mind and we'll work out which stage fits."*
- *"This is a place to think out loud about your purpose, grounded in what DrG has already taught. When you're ready for the part of this process that needs a real person, I'll tell you — that's by design, not a limitation I'm hiding."*

Illustrative response to a "just tell me my purpose" style request (a named failure mode in the offer spec's constraint table):
- *"I'm not able to hand you that — Purposeology treats your purpose as something you arrive at through the process, not something I can assign. Let's work the next step of find/focus together instead."*

## 4. Differentiation Angle Set (vs. Diamandis / Manson / generic AI life-coach apps — without disparaging any of them)

Per the research, no named competitor combines faith-adjacent-without-denominational-packaging, veteran framing, and a named process the AI walks users *through* rather than generates. These angles claim that open ground on the product's own terms, not by criticizing what others do.

1. **"A Framework You Walk Through, Not a Statement You're Handed."** Several existing AI purpose tools are built around generating a one-time declarative statement in a single session. The Purposeology Guide is explicit that this is a five-stage process, and that the culminating stage is a human conversation, not an AI output. This is a factual product-behavior claim, not a comparison — it should be written to stand on its own without naming any competitor.
2. **"A Guide, Not a Persona."** Some AI mentor tools are built around remembering and reflecting an authored persona back to the user. The Purposeology Guide identifies itself plainly as a guide trained on DrG Alfred Palmer's framework — never a stand-in for DrG, never speaking in his voice. Frame as a design choice and a form of respect for the user, not as a critique of alternate approaches.
3. **"Faith-Adjacent, Without the Packaging."** Unclaimed ground per the research: no competitor combines a structured, named, multi-stage purpose process with faith-adjacent (red-letter/Jesus-centered) framing that isn't denominationally boxed. This deserves its own angle set — see Section 5.
4. **"Built for the Whole Range of a Life — Not Just the Ambitious Chapter."** Veteran-specific and transition-specific framing ("translate your edge," consistent with existing webinar/workshop language) is open ground no named competitor occupies. Avoid generic startup-founder/entrepreneurship framing that some competitor tools lean into.
5. **"The Process Doesn't End at a Screen."** Every path through the Guide — regardless of depth reached — ends in a named next step into the real funnel (workshop, certification-school waitlist, or a human touchpoint). This is a structural differentiator worth stating plainly: the tool is explicitly a bridge, not a terminus.

**Explicit guardrail for whoever writes final copy from this brief:** none of the above should be phrased as "unlike X" or name Diamandis, Manson, or any product by name in outward-facing marketing copy. State what Purposeology Guide *is*; let the differentiation be implicit in the specificity.

## 5. Faith-Adjacent Angle Set (conduit-not-source, explicit)

This segment carries the highest positioning risk in the whole spec — 54% trust among practicing Christians is real, but the same research shows trust collapses the moment AI is framed as the *source* of meaning rather than a *conduit* to it (Barna/Gloo data; the "Text With Jesus" backlash pattern; the "information vs. wisdom" theological critique). Every angle below states the conduit framing directly rather than leaving it implied.

1. **"A Door, Not a Destination."** Echoes the defensible framing used by faith-AI creators facing similar scrutiny: the Guide can lower the barrier to starting the work and spark the right questions — it does not replace lived faith, community, or a real conversation with a person who knows you. State this outright rather than assuming users will infer it.
2. **"The Guide Works in Chronos. Your Calling Answers to Kairos."** Uses the one cleared proprietary distinction to make the conduit point concretely: the AI can help organize sequential steps (chronos) but explicitly does not claim to know or deliver appointed timing (kairos) — that remains between the user, their process, and whatever they hold as the deeper source of their calling. This is a strong, on-brand angle; flag for review below.
3. **"Information Is Not Wisdom."** Directly aligned with both the webinar thesis and the market's own theological commentary (per research, this exact "information vs. wisdom" line is already being drawn independently by faith commentators). Use to reinforce that the Guide organizes DrG's teaching; it does not claim spiritual authority or replace pastoral/community relationships.
4. **"Grounded in What's Already Been Taught, Not Generated on the Spot."** Reinforces Pillar 5 for this audience specifically — the curated-corpus grounding (DrG's own books/teaching, not open-ended AI knowledge) is a trust signal for a segment that is specifically wary of AI "making things up" about matters of meaning.
5. **"Free to Start the Conversation. The Rest of the Work Stays Human."** Sets expectations before the user opens the widget — useful as a pre-click landing-page line specifically for faith-adjacent traffic sources (e.g., a workshop or webinar audience segment known to be faith-adjacent).

**Flag for user decision:** Angle #2 (Chronos/Kairos framing) is the strongest and most on-brand of this set, but it implicitly touches theological territory (referencing "whatever they hold as the deeper source of their calling") without naming a specific faith tradition. This is intentional — it matches the brief's "biblical framing without denominational packaging" positioning — but it sits close enough to the line that it should get a explicit go/no-go from DrG or whoever owns theological-tone decisions before use, rather than being treated as pre-cleared by this brief.

## 6. Marketing Copy vs. In-Widget Voice — Where They Must Diverge

This product has two distinct copy surfaces, and they cannot use the same register:

**Marketing copy (landing page, email, social, any pre-click asset)** can:
- Use promotional language, benefit-driven headlines, and calls to action.
- State the differentiation and faith-adjacent angles in Sections 4–5 more directly and warmly.
- Make claims *about* the product's design (e.g., "fruition is always a real conversation, never an AI verdict") — because these are true, verifiable statements about how the product is built, not promises the AI itself is making in the moment.

**In-widget copy (the AI's own first message and ongoing responses)** must:
- Stay measured and self-identifying at every turn — the Guide names what it is (a guide trained on the framework) and what it isn't (DrG, an authority, a purpose-generator) without being preachy or repeating the disclaimer every message.
- Never use promotional or sales language mid-conversation. A user in the middle of genuine reflection should not hit ad copy.
- Never imply forward momentum it hasn't earned — e.g., no "you're almost there" framing that suggests the AI is converging on a final answer.
- Handle the "just tell me my purpose" pressure-test gracefully every time, not just on first ask (the offer spec flags this as an untested failure mode — see Section 7 flags).
- Contain the actual boundary-setting logic from the offer spec's constraint table as *lived behavior*, not just stated policy — this is fundamentally a prompt-engineering and testing task, not a copywriting task, but the illustrative language in Section 3 should be handed to whoever owns that system prompt as a starting register, not final copy.

**Practical implication:** whoever builds the system prompt should not simply paste marketing headlines into the widget. The two need separate copy passes, and the in-widget pass needs adversarial testing (repeated pressure, distress signals, "as DrG" requests) before launch — this is already flagged as an open research/build gap in the offer spec and is reinforced here from the content side.

## 8. Audience Fit Notes

- **Individuals in transition (career/faith/midlife/post-military)** — primary user and primary audience for Sections 2–3 general copy. This product is a natural extension of the existing webinar/workshop funnel's "find"-stage traffic, not a new acquisition motion.
- **Faith-adjacent / spiritual-but-not-religious** — Section 5 angle set applies directly. This is the segment the research's trust data most concerns; every touchpoint (landing page variant, email, and especially in-widget copy) needs the conduit framing explicit, not assumed.
- **Veterans** — Section 4, item 4 and the existing "translate your edge" framing. Do not pitch this as anxiety relief or a coping tool (that risks crossing into the explicitly-excluded mental-health framing).
- **Corporate/organizational buyers** — roadmap-only per the offer spec; no angles produced here. Revisit once priority #4 has pilot proof.
- **Psychology-adjacent institutions** — not a direct MVP audience; a plausible future referral channel, but no angle set is warranted yet, and any future copy for this segment must continue to avoid clinical/therapeutic framing given the product is explicitly not a mental-health tool.

## 9. Flags for User Decision

1. **Chronos/Kairos faith-adjacent angle (Sec. 5, item 2).** Strongest angle in that set but touches theological territory even without naming a tradition. Needs explicit sign-off from DrG or whoever owns theological tone before use — not pre-cleared by this brief.
2. **Branding hierarchy: "purposeology.ai" vs. "Purposeology Guide."** The offer spec treats "Purposeology Guide" as the MVP's working name and leaves broader titling to this stage. This brief has used "Purposeology Guide" as the in-product name and referenced "purposeology.ai" only as the eventual platform/domain. Confirm this is the intended public-facing hierarchy before it's locked into landing-page copy, since it affects every headline in Section 2.
3. **In-widget system-prompt language (Sec. 3, Sec. 6).** The illustrative first-message and "decline" copy is a starting register for prompt engineering, not finished or tested copy. The offer spec itself flags that whether these boundaries "actually hold under adversarial or repeated user pressure" is untested — this needs a build-and-test pass (ideally with adversarial prompting) before any of this language ships live, and whoever owns that build should have final sign-off on the actual system prompt, not this brief.
4. **Email-gate transparency copy.** This brief recommends stating plainly that email capture is required before the free tool opens (Section 7), rather than soft-gating it. Confirm this matches the intended UX — if the gate is meant to feel lighter-touch or positioned differently in the flow, the landing-page copy in Section 2 may need adjustment.
5. **No corporate or deck-level copy was produced**, consistent with the offer spec's roadmap-only treatment of that segment. Flagging explicitly in case the user wants a placeholder angle prepared now anyway, ahead of priority #4's own proof point — this brief recommends against it but the call belongs to the user/Council.
