## 3. Audience Pain Points — From the Booker's Side

Consistent findings across multiple event-planning and speaker-selection guides (2026 vintage):

- **Full-length video over sizzle reels.** Guidance consistently urges planners to request full 15–20 minute recordings of recent talks (not just a highlight reel) to assess story depth, Q&A handling, and — critically — to "watch the audience, not just the speaker" for genuine engagement in wide shots. A polished 2–3 minute sizzle reel is treated as necessary but insufficient. [eSpeakers](https://www.espeakers.com/how-to-use-speaker-reviews-and-videos-to-select-the-right-speaker/)
- **Verified references over curated testimonials.** Guides explicitly distinguish "testimonials on a speaker's website are curated" from "references are real" — planners are advised to request contact info for planners who booked the same speaker for a similar event/audience, not rely on a testimonials page. This is a repeatable, sourced distinction worth taking seriously for how DrG's own site presents social proof.
- **Audience/topic fit and customization willingness matter more than fame.** Multiple sources state explicitly that "the best keynote speaker is not necessarily the most famous or most expensive, but the one whose message fits the audience at their specific moment," and that a speaker's willingness to customize content for a sector/audience is read as a professionalism signal.
- **Price transparency is inconsistent across bureaus** (see the Willie Jolley fee-spread finding above) — several sources note bureaus commonly list fees "available upon request" for faith/leadership-tier speakers rather than posting a number, which is standard industry practice at this tier, not a red flag specific to any one speaker.
[Speakers.com selection guide](https://speakers.com/the-definitive-checklist-for-selecting-a-keynote-speaker-at-your-conference/), [Jonathan Dunnett](https://jonathandunnett.com/what-event-planners-look-for-speakers/)

**Read:** For DrG specifically, the actionable implication is that video quality (full-length, audience-reaction-visible) and *verifiable* (not just curated) testimonials/references from past bookers — ideally naming the conference/organization — will matter more to bookers than fame or a large media footprint. This is squarely within offer-architect/content-angle-strategist's remit to act on, not this report's to design.

## 5. Timing/Channel Signals

- **Booking lead times (2026 industry guidance, general, not niche-specific):** established/professional-tier speakers are typically booked **3–6 months** in advance; premium/global-figure tier requires **6–12 months** (up to 18 months for elite/global icons); emerging speakers operate in a more flexible 3–6-month window. International bookings need an added 2–3 months for visa/logistics. [Speakers.com](https://speakers.com/the-strategic-timeline-for-booking-a-conference-speaker-a-master-planners-guide/)
- **Seasonal competition pattern flagged across sources:** a "Q1 Kickoff" booking rush and an "October Conference Season" are both cited as periods of intense competition that can exhaust premier-speaker availability up to a year in advance — useful for planning outreach/pitch timing to land on bookers' calendars before those crunch windows.
- **Confirmed, dated 2026 events in DrG's exact circuits** (useful direct outreach targets, not endorsements):
