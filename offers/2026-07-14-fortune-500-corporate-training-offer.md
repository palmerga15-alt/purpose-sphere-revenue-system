## Offer Name

**Enterprise Purpose & Leadership Engagement** (working name — titles/positioning language are content-angle-strategist's job, not architected here)

## Summary

A corporate training engagement sold to enterprise L&D/HR/talent-development leadership, built around a single differentiated pitch the webinar research directly surfaced: **2026 corporate AI-training investment is real and large ($400B+ per Josh Bersin) but almost entirely skills/productivity framed (72% efficiency, 70% future-skills) — virtually no vendor is selling AI adoption as a meaning-and-purpose-retention problem.** This offer positions Purposeology™ as the layer that makes an organization's AI rollout something employees stay engaged and purposeful through, not just something they're trained to operate — directly parallel to the webinar's "AI gives you information, Purposeology gives you the reason to use it" thesis, translated to an organizational/retention register rather than an individual-anxiety one.

This is explicitly **not** a general leadership-development or AI-skills-training offer. It does not compete on skills content (that lane is saturated per the research — Bersin's 72%/70% figures describe exactly the crowded space this offer avoids). It competes on the meaning/retention angle the research found is currently unclaimed.

## Who It's For

Maps to the brief's existing "Corporate/organizational buyers — leadership training, Fortune 500 L&D" segment — no new segment is introduced, but the buyer persona is specified here for the first time:

- **Primary buyer/decision-maker:** CHRO, VP/Head of Talent Development, VP/Head of L&D — the budget-holder for leadership and culture programs, distinct from IT/technical-training buyers who own AI-skills budgets.
- **Champion/entry point:** often an internal L&D program manager or a CHRO-adjacent exec who has already seen the AI-skills-training wave inside their org and is looking for something that addresses retention/engagement fallout, not more tool training.
- **End participants:** mid-to-senior leadership cohorts (director level and above) inside a target account, not front-line/individual-contributor populations — consistent with a "purpose-and-leadership" framing rather than a mass-rollout skills product.

## Format / Duration

Three tiers, ordered by commitment level, each usable as a standalone entry point:

1. **Discovery/Executive Session** — a single 60–90 minute working session with an L&D/HR leadership team (not a mainstage keynote — this is a working conversation, typically the outcome of a priority #3 keynote booking or a direct corporate inbound). Purpose: diagnose the org's specific meaning/retention gap around its current AI rollout and scope a larger engagement. Framed as a paid consulting session or, in early pilot cases, a complimentary scoping call in exchange for case-study rights (see Minimal Proof Point below).
2. **Leadership Training Day** — a single on-site or virtual full-day (or two half-days) engagement with a defined leadership cohort. The core "training day" format most comparable to a standard corporate L&D purchase.
3. **Multi-Session Enterprise Engagement** — a structured program (e.g., 3–6 sessions across a quarter or two) for a leadership cohort or multiple business units, with between-session application work. This is the format that most plausibly sustains an ongoing/annual relationship and is where the Corporate Delivery License bench (priority #2) becomes economically necessary rather than optional.

All three reuse the same underlying five-stage journey structure (find, focus, form, follow, fruition) already used across every other offer in this pipeline — reframed here as an organizational lens (what is this AI rollout *for*, what does purposeful adoption look like for this leadership team, how do we sustain it) rather than an individual-calling lens. No internal 26-concept framework or Concept Dictionary content is described or implied.

## Deliverables and Outcomes

Framed around the five-stage journey, organizational register, no internal concepts named:

- Leadership participants leave a session or engagement able to name, for their own team, **what their organization's AI adoption is actually for** — a *find*-stage reframe applied to a business initiative rather than an individual's life question.
- A defined, team-specific articulation of what purposeful (not just efficient) AI adoption looks like for their function — a *focus*-and-*form*-stage output, delivered as a concrete team artifact (e.g., a stated adoption principle or team commitment), not a completed change-management plan.
- For multi-session engagements: a *follow*-stage cadence — structured check-ins or embedded practices that sustain the reframe past the initial session, which is the piece a single training day cannot deliver and the reason the multi-session tier exists.
- The organization itself receives a documented engagement (attendance, testimonial rights where negotiated, and — critically — the case-study material this whole offer needs to move past its pilot stage, see below).
- **Fruition-stage outcome claim:** engagement participants report a stronger connection between AI-adoption effort and personal/team meaning — this is the retention-adjacent outcome the offer is built to produce, but it is a design intent, not a measured result yet (no pilot has run). Any specific retention/engagement metric claim (e.g., "X% improvement") would be unfounded until a pilot produces real data and should not appear in marketing copy without it.

## Delivery Model: DrG Personally vs. the Corporate Delivery License Bench

- **Initial delivery (near-term, pilot phase): DrG delivers personally.** This is the only credible delivery model until a pilot exists — there is no track record, no case study, and no trained delivery bench yet. DrG's personal delivery is also what a first enterprise buyer would expect and vet for at this stage (a founder-delivered engagement is more credible pre-proof than a delegated one).
- **Scaled delivery (later, once proof exists): the Corporate Delivery License bench, per priority #2.** The certification-school offer spec already defines this mechanism explicitly: certified practitioners who hold the Corporate Delivery License tier ($12,000/yr scaling to $25,000+/yr per practitioner/account) are the intended staffing/delivery arm for exactly this kind of engagement, because DrG cannot personally staff Fortune 500-scale, multi-account delivery. **This mechanism does not exist yet** — the certification-school spec recommends only a small pilot cohort before building the licensing/legal/quality-control infrastructure the Corporate Delivery License requires. This offer's multi-session/enterprise tier is therefore **dependent on priority #2 reaching at least a functioning licensed-practitioner pilot before it can scale beyond DrG's own calendar** — a real sequencing dependency, not a formality.

## Explicit Council Guidance Flag — New-Build-Front Assessment

**This is arguably the most new-front-heavy priority in the pipeline so far — more so than #2 or #3.**

- Priority #2 (certification school) reuses an existing content structure (the five-stage journey) sold through an existing warm funnel (the webinar/workshop waitlist); its main new-build items are curriculum, an LMS, and an assessment process — real work, but a direct-to-consumer sales motion the org already runs.
- Priority #3 (speaking) reuses an existing personal capability (DrG can already speak, has a documented platform history) and mainly needs packaging (a one-sheet, video, references) — its own spec explicitly frames this as "asset-reuse-first," not new capability-building.
- **Priority #4 requires all of the above (new curriculum, a delivery bench that does not exist, a differentiation thesis with no pilot yet) *plus* an enterprise sales motion this organization has never run before** — longer buying cycles, multi-stakeholder decision-making, procurement/legal infrastructure, and a delivery model (the Corporate Delivery License bench) that is itself downstream of an unbuilt piece of priority #2. This stacks new-build risk on top of new-build risk in a way #2 and #3 individually do not.

This assessment should weigh directly against the Purpose Council's stated preference to strengthen proof and delivery infrastructure before opening new fronts. Treating this as a fully resourced build-out now — dedicated enterprise sales hire, formal MSA/legal work, marketed pricing tiers — would be premature. A narrower, opportunistic pilot motion is the more defensible near-term action.

## Recommended Minimal Proof Point

Given DrG has no Fortune 500 engagement today, the following is the lowest-cost, lowest-risk path to real proof, in priority order:

1. **Identify a single pilot account through an existing relationship, not cold enterprise outreach.** Candidates already implied by the brief itself: the psychology-adjacent institutions named as an "existing cold outreach target" (mental health clinics, universities, corporate wellness programs), the NuHorizons/veteran-services connection, or any corporate contact surfaced through the priority #3 speaking pipeline (e.g., a sponsor or attendee organization at a veteran-services or Black-leadership circuit event). A true Fortune 500 name is the aspiration, not a requirement for the pilot itself — a mid-size or large organization with a real AI-adoption initiative underway is sufficient to test the thesis.
2. **Use the priority #3 speaking offer as the door-opener, exactly as that spec already anticipates.** A booked keynote at a corporate event is lower-friction than a cold enterprise-training pitch; the post-keynote conversation with the booking organization's L&D/HR contact is the natural entry into a Discovery/Executive Session.
3. **Price the pilot to generate proof, not maximum fee** — a complimentary or below-placeholder-rate Discovery/Executive Session and/or Leadership Training Day, explicitly traded for documented case-study rights, a named reference, and (if possible) a real before/after engagement-or-retention data point tied to the AI-adoption initiative. This mirrors the speaking offer's own "Strategic/relationship bookings" logic — proof-asset generation over near-term revenue for the first 1–2 engagements.
4. **Do not build the multi-session/enterprise-license tier, the Corporate Delivery License bench, or any enterprise sales infrastructure (MSA templates, bureau-style representation, dedicated collateral) until the pilot validates that the meaning/retention pitch actually lands with a real corporate buyer.** The webinar research shows the *market gap* exists (skills/productivity-only framing dominates); it does not show that *this specific pitch, from this specific vendor, at these specific price points* converts a real enterprise buyer. That is what the pilot is for.
